Backdoor

An Original Scenario written by Akira

I sneak in through the back entrance. I feel like some ridiculously cool outlaw straight out of a movie. Dodging countless traps, I open fire on the enemies, mowing them down one after another! Bang! Bang! Bang! How do you like that, you idiots of the world?!

I am the great Oogami Koga…!

"..."

Standing beside the back entrance is some flashy-looking blond guy who seems to be working part-time. He's busy tying up garbage bags, but when he spots me, a middle schooler who's just barged in and started acting like a weirdo, he gives me a surprised look.

“Hey, you–” Part-time-kun (tentative name) holds his hand out with a totally bored expression. “One thousand yen. That’s the admission fee.”

“Oh, right. Sorry.”

“Next time, make sure you come in through the proper entrance, ‘kay?” Part-time-kun (tentative name) doesn't bother lecturing me any further. Instead, he simply takes my thousand-yen bill and hands me a strangely stylish plastic card in exchange. Apparently, I can exchange it at the counter for a drink or something. Well, thanks for the helpful explanation.

“Enjoy yourself～♪”

Watching Part-Time-kun (tentative name) leave through the back entrance, I realise that I’ve already embarrassed myself so I just want to turn around and go home. But I pull myself together and head inside.

I’m in a cheap underground livehouse in the downtown area, near Yumenosaki Academy, the school I’ll be attending.

This is my temple.

My God is here.

***

The underground livehouse I've been spending so much time at lately has a weird layout.

In an ordinary back alley in the entertainment district, there's a staircase leading down underground.

At the bottom, corridors stretch out to the left and right, lined with one soundproof door after another.

Most of them lead to small rooms, or booths, that amateur bands and the like can rent out for practice. I don’t think they don't get much use, because whenever I peer through the little windows, they're almost always empty.

Well, I suppose people don't really practise guitar the old-fashioned way these days. You know, actually picking up an instrument and playing until their fingers hurt.

Nowadays, they probably just sit at home, staring at their computer screens all leisurely like. I dunno, though.

At the far end of those gloomy corridors are the staff room, the kitchen, and the back entrance I'd stumbled upon by accident when I got caught trespassing.

That back entrance leads straight outside, and since there's usually nobody around, I've been using it to sneak in without paying the admission fee. Especially lately, when I've been running short on cash from buying beginner guitar books and stuff.

“Guess I won't be able to pull that trick again,” I grumble as I trudge dejectedly down the corridor, eventually reaching the soundproof door I'm looking for.

It's the biggest, fanciest door in the middle of the corridor.

Beyond it, at the very heart of this place, is the livehouse, or, well, you could call it a music hall.

“♪～♪～♪”

The moment I open the heavy door, a wall of sound crashes into me, enveloping my entire body.

This is what I'm talking about. This electrifying feeling.

At the far end of the fairly spacious room is a pretty impressive stage, where bands that have applied to perform at the venue put on their shows.

Customers pay an admission fee that includes the price of a drink, then enjoy the performances while having whatever they've ordered. Just your average livehouse, I guess. Not that I'd know.

I’m underaged, so I stick to a non-alcoholic tomato juice whilst enjoying the show.

Maybe because it's so close to Yumenosaki, a lot of the customers are students with scruffy appearances. And since I'm young myself, I don't really stand out as a customer at some run-down livehouse like this. Besides, I'm just wearing my usual clothes.

Maybe the management takes its clientele into consideration, because there's hardly any smell of alcohol or cigarettes.

Only super cool music fills the place. It reverberates all around me.

“～...♪”

Right in the middle of the stage, my God is singing his heart out. The lyrics are in English, and I can barely understand a word.

He's probably singing about wishing for world peace, or maybe blaspheming against God. Something complicated but meaningful, no doubt.

Though, when I asked him about it later, apparently the lyrics were something along the lines of, “I'm so happy! My adorable little brother's finally gotten over his cold!”

What an idiot. No, I’m the idiot for being so entranced by him.

But. I didn’t know that sort of thing back then, so I was genuinely moved by him.

There he stood, bathed in the faint glow of a dimmed spotlight, his corpse-pale skin standing out against the surrounding darkness.

Him.

Crimson eyes, like the raging fires of hell.

Black hair, seemingly melting into the shadows.

And from lips more alluring than those of even the most seductive woman came a deep, powerful bass voice, unmistakably masculine.

He looked like an angel capable of saving the world, and at the same time, a devil who could bring about its destruction.

Either way, his singing voice possessed the power to effortlessly reshape the entire world.

“～...♪”

The name of the person I respect more than anyone else in this world is Sakuma Rei.

My God.

***

I grew up in a pretty ordinary household.

Your typical middle-class family. My dad's a salaryman with a fairly decent income, and my mum's a full-time housewife, which seems to be pretty rare these days.

They live in a fairly nice house in a fairly nice neighbourhood.

Both of my parents love kids and enjoy looking after people, so I grew up being thoroughly spoiled and fussed over.

I'm well aware that being pampered like that turned me into a selfish, cocky brat

I was given everything I wanted. In fact, things were always given to me before I even had the chance to want them, so I never really learned what it was I truly wanted for myself.

Once I was old enough that I didn't need looking after quite so much, my parents got a dog to satisfy their seemingly endless desire to take care of someone.

His name's Leon. Best dog ever.

I doted on him, too. I mean, I practically treated him like a baby (even though he's a dog), but my parents took things to a whole other level. Every single day, they showered him with so much affection that it made me think they must've treated me the same way when I was a baby.

I got a little sulky.

Because I could tell that my parents' attention had shifted from me to Leon, and they weren't exactly subtle about it.

But Leon wasn't to blame. He'd been brought into the family to be loved.

He's a pedigree dog, born to be doted on and adored.

And he really was the sweetest thing. Whenever I felt lonely, he'd snuggle up beside me and lick my face, as if to tell me I wasn't alone.

That I didn't have to feel lonely.

But I could feel, almost physically, how much the affection I'd always received had dwindled. And it made me anxious.

—Ah, what a pathetic, spoiled little Oogami Koga-kun!

But I wasn't shameless enough to actually say, “Forget the dog! Pay attention to me instead!” Besides, I felt that Leon deserved to be loved just as much as I did. So I started wandering around town, searching for someone other than my parents who could love me.

I was starving. Thirsty, too.

I was like a stray dog wandering around in search of something to fill its belly.

My parents weren't bad people. Leon wasn't bad. I’m probably not bad either.

I was nearing the end of my compulsory schooling. I'd reached the age when I was supposed to be able to fend for myself.

So naturally, that's what I should've done. I'm sure everyone else was doing the same.

We leave the protection of our parents, make our way through adolescence, and find out who we really are.

We find our own lives.

And after making one stupid attempt after another, fumbling around until I no longer knew which way to turn, I finally found what I'd been looking for.

Sakuma Rei.

His music breathed life back into my utterly parched soul.

***

The performance ends, and Sakuma Rei disappears backstage.

I've never been onstage before, so I don't really know what things are like back there. I imagine there's probably some sort of passageway leading to a dressing room or something.

The livehouse is weirdly dark and it’s hard to see much of anything, so it really looks like Sakuma Rei vanished like a ghost.

The one thing that quenches my thirst, that fills the emptiness inside me, disappears.

And so I grow anxious again. With no particular destination in mind, I begin wandering around in search of him.

I weave my way through the crowds of people who've gathered here to see Sakuma Rei, wandering aimlessly around the venue.

—Sakuma Rei. Sakuma Rei. Sakuma Rei.

My soul yearns for him.

Of course, I'm not his family or anything. We're not even acquaintances, let alone friends.

He probably doesn't even know I exist.

And that was fine with me. I'd found him, encountered him, fallen in love with him, come to admire him, and found fulfilment in that. I was grateful just for that much. Sakuma Rei had undoubtedly saved me.

I had no intention of asking for anything more.

I was just one of the countless starstruck fans gathered here. To me, the place where that man stood was as distant as the night sky. I never imagined I could reach him.

Just watching him from afar was enough.

That was how I truly felt. It was more than enough. If anything, I'd already been given more than I could ever ask for.

And yet.

“Oh, that looks good. What're you drinking?”

Without warning, someone beside me takes the glass of tomato juice right out of my hand. I'd ordered it on a whim, but it turned out to be weirdly sweet, and I'd already gone off it.

—The hell, asshole? You wanna go? That's mine!

I glare up at the culprit, ready for a fight.

And there stands Sakuma Rei.

“If you’re not drinking it, I’ll have it. Singing’s got me workin' up a sweat.”

Naturally, my entire body stiffens.

That’s Sakuma Rei.

He's so close I could reach out and touch him. Hell, we're practically pressed up against each other.

I'd never experienced anything like this before. I was so overwhelmed by something I'd never even dreamed possible that I couldn't think of a single clever thing to say. I just stood there like an idiot, my mouth hanging open.

“What’s up? Oh, you’re at that age where you think indirect kisses are embarrassing, right…?” Sakuma Rei looks a little apologetic as he says this, but what comes out of his mouth is complete nonsense.

“Oh, nooo, I'm sorry~... But don't worry. I'll take responsibility for stealing your first time, kiddo. Mm-hmm.”

That was the first conversation I ever had with him. Not exactly something I can brag about to anyone.

Ever since then, ever since that moment, I’ve been at the mercy of this completely shameless person.

***

I step through the back entrance.

The blond employee, who looks as unmotivated as ever, is slacking off and messing around on his phone again today. He's actually the manager of this place, and apparently one of my senpai at Yumenosaki, too. Some playboy called Hakaze. He side-eyes me, pulling an absolutely revolting face as he taps away at his phone.

“Look, look! I just got another girl's number. I'm sending her my first message right now.”

“Shut up, I don’t know you. Don’t talk to me, playboy.”

It's been about two years since my first encounter with Sakuma Rei – Sakuma-senpai. An encounter I'd really rather not remember.

I've grown a little taller and a little stronger.

I've practised like crazy, so my guitar playing and singing have gotten a little better, at least.

And while I was making those small strides of my own, Yumenosaki had gone to the dogs.

Yumenosaki Academy. An idol-training school steeped in a glorious history and rich tradition.

But beneath that shining exterior, it was rotten to the core.

I'd wanted to be like Sakuma-senpai, so I'd blindly followed in his footsteps and taken the entrance exam for Yumenosaki without giving it a second thought. What an idiot I was.

I was blinded. I didn’t know anything.

The students at Yumenosaki were all scum.

I should've realised that, of course. After all, I'd been coming to this underground live house again and again just to see Sakuma-senpai.

A rotten world where people with dead eyes spent their dreary youth, surrounded by the stench of death.

Sakuma-senpai had seemed strangely full of life despite that environment, so I'd gotten the wrong idea.

No. I think I was just an immature, stupid kid who couldn't see what was right in front of him.

Sakuma-senpai had the same dead eyes as everyone else.

He was a living corpse, surrounded by mountains of bodies, wishing that he could die himself but somehow unable to. All he could do was pray that something interesting would happen.

Nobody could save him.

A bespectacled monk from a temple came along and incurred his wrath by trying to turn him into something other than human, more like, something greater than human.

And that stupid stray dog who'd wandered into the picture just kept wagging his tail, delighted to be fed.

We never realised that the man who was always grinning like an idiot and seemed to be living such a happy life was actually suffering more than anyone else, desperately crying out for help.

But, you know, he always looked like he was having fun.

When he stood onstage with me and Shitty Glasses, during that brief period when we called ourselves Deadmanz and performed together on a whim, he looked genuinely happy.

He looked alive.

But that was only a fleeting dream.

The dream ended, the magic wore off, and he turned back into a corpse.

A revolution takes place at the rotted Yumenosaki.

Sakuma-senpai was branded as evil and exterminated by the self-proclaimed heroes of justice who'd risen up to bring about that revolution.

The evil monsters were defeated, and everyone lived happily ever after.

Yeah. I guess it's pretty creepy when a corpse starts moving.

You've gotta get rid of those disgusting monsters, right?

—You fucking bastards!

***

“Wanko, are you performing again today?”

The playboy asks, fiddling with his phone as usual, sounding completely uninterested.

I told you not to talk to me, I think.

“Could you maybe stop? You're kind of bringing down the mood around here. I mean, people think you're one of Sakuma-san's lackeys, right? And since his lackeys must be bad guys too, everyone's been treating you like some kind of villain.”

“I don't care. I... I, the great Koga...” I growl, letting the frivolous first-person pronoun Sakuma-senpai occasionally used slip from my lips. I cling onto what I’ve got left of him. “I just wanna sing with everything I've got. The rest of those guys have nothin’ to do with me.”

“But it's a problem for the livehouse. Some customer starts making fun of you or Sakuma-san, you lose your temper, and before you know it, there's a massive brawl. I really don't want anything like that happening here.”

“I won't cause any trouble. I'll be on my best behaviour.”

“Really? But you always come in through the back entrance to avoid trouble with the other customers. Yumenosaki students, I mean. Because you're worried you'll end up getting into a fight with them, right? Every single time?”

“I still pay the admission fee, don't I?”

“That's what I'm asking! Why go out of your way to perform at our place when it means taking such a dangerous risk? It's a real pain, you know.” The playboy grumbles, but pulls out a key and tosses it over to me.

“Here. I'll lend you a room. You can change into your stage outfit and get ready in there. As long as you promise not to cause any trouble, you're actually becoming a pretty decent source of income for us, Wanko.”

“Truth is, I kinda wanna root for you,” the playboy adds with a flippant laugh.

His attitude pisses me off, so I snap at him.

“Don't call me Wanko.”

“But Sakuma-san used to call you Wanko, didn't he? Besides, when I say ‘Wan’, I mean number one! Seriously, I mean it!” For just a moment, the playboy's expression turns serious. Then he starts waving his hands around, as if trying to hide how embarrassed he is. “Become the number-one money-maker here, just like Sakuma-san.”

“Don’t hafta tell me twice.”

I'll become a man just like Sakuma-senpai. The greatest man in the world.

I'll set the whole crowd ablaze just by singing. One sultry glance, and the girls will swoon.

I'll only have to look a man in the eye and speak to him, and even the toughest guys will melt in my hands.

In the blink of an eye, I'll have their souls in my grasp. Everyone will be captivated.

I'll become a man just like Sakuma Rei. I've still got an awfully long way to go, though.

“Let's go. I'm performing tonight.”

I reach my booth, key in hand, and change into my costume. Then I take out my guitar, my precious partner, just as important to me as my parents and Leon.

Once I've taken care of everything and finished getting ready, I head for the stage. To tackle this head on.

“Shake, you fools! Imma show you what real music is!”

I sing. I strum my guitar with all my might.

Just like Sakuma-senpai once did.

For now, I'm nothing but an imitation. But I pray that someday, I'll become just like him.

And I hope my song reaches him, too, wherever he might be now.

***

Once, I was starving. Thirsty, too.

But when I found Sakuma Rei and his music, my parched soul was finally replenished.

—Now it's my turn.

“Rock 'n' roll...!”

Come on, you idiots of the world.

I'll make you listen to my music.

I'll become your God.
