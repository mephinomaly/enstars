Velvet Ship

Haul To The Wind / Chapter 3

Time: Flashback to two weeks prior

Location: In the Rhythm Link Meeting Space

Koga: Ah. Sakuma-senpai and Adonis are already here.

…What’s with this atmosphere? Are they havin’ some kinda serious conversation?

I can’t exactly butt in… Hakaze-senpai isn’t here yet, either.

Adonis: —I see. There are certainly complicated aspects to it but I think I just about understand.

Japanese is a beautifully complex and fascinating language. The more I learn, the more rewarding it becomes.

Rei: Indeed. Continue to approach your studies with enthusiasm, and strive to improve yourself everyday.

Adonis-kun is a diligent student, after all. Perhaps one day, you may even catch up to me ♪

Adonis: I am not yet as skilled as you, so I do not believe that to be possible.

But as a member of the same unit, I want to become stronger. I want to reach the point where I can stand beside you as an equal.

Rei: How admirable. I shall look forward to the day...♪

Koga: ...What the hell are you two talkin’ about?

Adonis: Ah, Oogami, you’re here. We were discussing strawberry picking.

Koga: Strawberry picking…?

Rei: Indeed. Last month, Adonis-kun’s dormmate invited him to accompany them.

He approached the outing with considerable zeal, but when all they did was pick strawberries, he was somewhat disappointed.

Koga: The hell? I thought you were havin’ some kinda serious conversation, but you’ve jus’ been talkin’ about strawberries?

Rei: Oya? Were you, perhaps, eavesdropping on our conversation? Koga is a naughty boy.

Koga: I wasn’t! I jus’ overheard you, that’s all!

I thought you were havin’ a serious conversation, so it was kinda awkward tryna find a good time to jump in.

Adonis: ...Is that so? You waited until I was down speaking so as to not interrupt me. Thank you, Oogami.

Koga: Stop bein’ so damn polite.

Anyway, why were you disappointed? It kinda does what it says on the tin.

Adonis: ...Umu. It was described to me as a strawberry hunt, so I assumed it would involve hunting.

The day before, I exercised so I would be in peak physical condition. I also went to bed early to ensure that I would be properly rested.

However, when we arrived, I discovered that a strawberry hunt simply involves picking and eating strawberries.

I found picking them to be surprisingly difficult. You have to be very careful not to crush them.

It had nothing to do with hunting like I had expected. Though, I suppose it did provide a different form of training.

Kaoru: Right... How the hell did you even come t’that conclusion? Didn’t the word strawberry make you think something was off?

Adonis: Not particularly. In Japanese, the word for venison can also be read as momiji, which is also the word for autumn foliage. I assumed that ichigo-gari followed the same principle. [1]

Rei: Kukuku. I was merely giving him a thorough explanation as to why we refer to it as ichigo-gari.

There are several theories regarding its origins, so it is difficult to say that any single explanation is definitively correct.

But that is exactly what makes language so fascinating. The fact that a word can have so many theories surrounding its origins is proof of how deeply it has become woven into the very fabric of our culture.

By tracing the history and background of our words, we can catch glimpses of the lives and values of the people who came before us.

Language is a thread that connects the past with the present.

Koga: Tch. You’re jus’ stringin’ together a bunch of fancy words. I didn’t come here to get lectured.

Kaoru: —It’s all part of the job, right?

Sorry sorry! The elevator decided to stop at every single floor so I ended up being later than I expected.

Have you guys already started the meeting? Soz, we’re supposed to be talking about being grateful, and I show up late. Embarrassing~

Koga: Huh? Grateful?

You show up late ‘n start spoutin’ random shit. Were you dropped as a baby?

Adonis: I think I understand, somewhat. I believe he means that we should be grateful to our friends who graciously pass along the gift of knowledge. Just like Sakuma-senpai was earlier.

But it is not only Sakum-senpai. I learn from Oogami and Hakaze-senpai everyday as well.

Thank you, both of you.

Kaoru: You're welcome, I think…?

I don’t quite think we’re on the same page here.

Have you two not heard about the fan appreciation event that ES is holding? I thought that’s what today’s meeting was about.

Koga: Huh? I haven’t heard anythin’ about that.

Adonis: Same here. Were you aware of it, Sakuma-senpai?

…Sakuma-senpai? Why are you looking away?

Koga: Dude, don’t tell me—

Rei: Now, now. Surely you do not think that I, the demon king who rules over the darkness of night, would make such a trivial mistake?

Kaoru: Ahaha. It’s written all over your face, Rei-kun. You totally forgot, didn’t you?

Rei: Ough…

Koga: It’s obvious man. C’mon, jus’ spit it out already.

Rei: Good grief… Very well, this old man shall confess.

I had originally intended to explain everything to each of you in turn. However, after explaining it to Kaoru-kun, I lost focus. It seems the fact that I had yet to inform the two of you simply slipped my mind.

Kaoru: This guy…

Do you know what a phone? It’s this handy-dandy little invention that lets you tell people information without even needing to speak with them in person ♪

Rei: Come now, have some mercy. I may not be particularly adept with the latest technology, but I can just about manage HoldHands.

The trouble is, I find typing such a tedious affair…

There was quite a lot I needed to explain this time, you see, so I knew you two would have no shortage of questions for me.

Young people are frightfully quick when it comes to replying. By the time I had answered one question, five more would have arrived. I simply could not hope to keep up.

So I thought it would be considerably faster to explain everything in person.

Koga: I get your excuse ‘n all, but I still don’t have a clue what you’re gettin’ at

What the hell is the ES fan appreciation event, anyway?

  1. nihongo lesson! 苺狩り (ichigo-gari) literally translates to “strawberry hunting,” but refers to strawberry picking. adonis takes the phrase literally, wondering if ichigo might mean something other than strawberries. to explain his reasoning, he points out that momiji can mean both “autumn leaves” and “venison” in japanese. so, in theory, momiji-gari could be interpreted as either hunting deer or viewing autumn leaves, though the phrase normally refers to the latter
